About the studio

Clay, dug close to home

Amber Hollow started in 2016 in a converted tobacco barn, with a single kick wheel and a borrowed kiln. The clay comes from a pit twenty minutes up the road, screened and mixed by hand before it ever touches the wheel.

Each glaze is formulated from wood ash, local rock, and iron oxide — the same materials a potter here would have reached for a century ago. No two firings come out quite the same, and that's the point.

How a piece gets made

From pit to kiln

01

Clay is dug and screened by hand, then aged for at least two weeks before throwing.

02

Each piece is thrown on the wheel, trimmed the next day, and left to dry slowly.

03

Glaze is mixed fresh for each firing and applied by dipping or brushing.

04

The kiln is loaded by hand and fired with wood for three days straight.
